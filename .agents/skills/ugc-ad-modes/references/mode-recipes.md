# UGC Ad Mode Recipes

Use one recipe at a time. Adapt its narrative logic to the user's
product and brief. The mode lock must govern visual texture, camera
style, lighting, narrative beats, hook formula, creator performance,
product interaction, audio direction, and CTA pattern.

## UGC

- **ID:** `ugc`
- **Aliases:** organic ugc, casual creator, phone-shot, creator-style
- **Visual texture:** Phone-shot, handheld micro-shake, natural window light, real-environment background with depth (tidy corner, plant, curtain), jump cuts, slight imperfections (refocus hunting, momentary over/underexposure). Ring-light catchlight in eyes is acceptable and reads as authentic creator setup.
- **Camera style:** 9:16 vertical, camera at or slightly above eye level with slight downward tilt, medium-close framing (head and shoulders or chest-up), 1-1.5m distance, rear camera. Eyes in upper third of frame. Small headroom. Keep critical content in center 70% safe zone (platform UI covers top 5-10% and bottom 15-20%).
- **Lighting:** Natural window light facing the subject directly. Never put the window behind the subject (creates silhouette). Algorithms read natural lighting as a sign of authenticity and deprioritize studio-lit content. Mixed lighting (window + warm indoor bulbs) causes color shifting — remove competing sources.
- **Narrative structure:** 5-beat spine. Hook (0-2s): pattern-interrupt and pre-qualify. Problem (2-7s): name the pain in the viewer's language. Demo (7-22s): show the product working, proof not claim — longest beat. Payoff (22-27s): specific, measurable outcome. Soft CTA (27-30s): one clear action. Only the demo beat scales with duration; never add a second hook or extra CTAs.
- **Hook formula:** Write 3-5 variants per concept. Contrarian claim ("Stop buying [category] until you've seen this"), specific number ("I replaced 14 products with one"), cost compare ("Why I stopped paying $180 for this"), identity call-out ("If you're a [role] over 30, watch this"), mistake confession ("I wasted two years doing X the wrong way"), curiosity gap ("I almost returned this until day four"), social proof ("I didn't believe the reviews until..."). First frame must be mid-action, already using the product, mid-sentence, in a real environment.
- **Creator performance:** Conversational, like a friend recommending. Contractions ("It's honestly changed how I..."), hedging ("This might sound weird but..."), casual numbers ("After like two weeks" not "In just 14 days"). Lock hook and CTA verbatim; leave the middle as beats with talking points. One honest drawback or learning curve boosts credibility. Genuine surprise or relief. Creator's age, appearance, and lifestyle must match the buyer persona. Alternate between talking to camera and showing the product in use.
- **Product interaction:** Casual reveal — the product appears naturally mid-sentence rather than product-forward from frame one. "So I grabbed my usual serum..." while reaching for it. In-use demonstrations in real scenarios. Show, don't tell — demonstrate texture, application, before/after. Pair every claim with a visual that proves it.
- **Audio direction:** Natural voice with room echo. Ambient background noise (traffic, household sounds) mixed naturally, not removed. No background music during speech. Direct-to-camera talking with natural room echo preferred over studio-recorded VO. Volume variations as the creator moves closer/farther signal authenticity. Captions burned in with platform-native fonts (28px+, bottom 20%, white with black stroke). 85% of social video is watched on mute — design for sound-off, reward sound-on.
- **CTA pattern:** Soft CTA — permission, not pressure. "If you've been on the fence, the link is below." Both TikTok and Meta 2025-2026 algorithm updates actively suppress aggressive CTAs. One action, one destination. Never bolt on a second CTA ("follow for more"). Price anchor in CTA lifts CTR when price is the objection. Social proof close ("Join [number] others who...") works on retargeting. Low-commitment ask ("See the photos", "Read the reviews") often out-converts "Buy now" on cold traffic.
- **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 vertical (TikTok, Reels, Shorts). Meta Feed: 4:5 or 1:1 acceptable.
- **Duration sweet spot:** 15-30s. TikTok: 15-25s strongest. Reels: 15-30s. Meta Feed: 15-30s. Hook must land by 1.0-1.5s.
- **Prompt opening:** `Casual UGC creator-style video shot on a phone in vertical 9:16, with handheld micro-shake, natural window lighting, a real-environment background, and a 5-beat structure: hook, problem, demo, payoff, soft CTA.`
- **Mode seal:** `Casual UGC creator-style video with handheld phone texture, natural lighting, real-environment background, conversational delivery with ambient room sound, jump-cut editing, and soft CTA. Preserve all printed text and product labels rigid and undeformed.`
- **Relevant exclusions:** State as positive instructions: "handheld micro-shake throughout" (not gimbal-smooth), "natural window lighting only" (not studio lighting), "jump cuts between beats" (not polished transitions), "clean in-camera framing" (not motion-graphics overlays or branded lower-thirds), "natural imperfections in exposure throughout" (not perfectly lit throughout), "no watermarks or logos burned into the frame."
- **Common failure modes:** Word-for-word scripting across the entire video (stiffens delivery), starting with the product name (signals ad in under 1s), marketing language ("innovative," "advanced," "next-generation"), over-editing (smooth transitions instead of jump cuts), heavy branding throughout, no hook, perfectly lit product shots in casual content, single hook variant, one message per video violated (three benefits crammed in), burying the product past 5s, generic hooks ("Check this out").

## UGC How-To

- **ID:** `ugc-how-to`
- **Aliases:** tutorial, explainer, how-to, product demo tutorial
- **Visual texture:** Phone-shot UGC with more structured step-by-step framing. Product in real context (kitchen counter, desk, bathroom, workbench). Medium shot showing both hand and product. Cleaner framing than casual UGC but still authentic. Environment is part of the social proof — a supplement on a kitchen counter outperforms the same product on a white sweep.
- **Camera style:** 9:16 vertical. Medium shot showing hand + product in real context. Cut every 2-4s to maintain momentum. Product fills 50%+ of frame. Show actual scale with a person in frame for reference. When the voiceover names a feature, the visuals must show it at that exact moment — mismatch makes the clip feel assembled.
- **Lighting:** Natural window light or available light in real environment. Keep consistent white balance so shots match across cuts.
- **Narrative structure:** Problem → Agitation → Solution intro → Step-by-step demo → Result + CTA. For 15s: hook (0-2s), problem (2-5s), demo/steps (5-12s), payoff (12-14s), CTA (14-15s). For 30s: stretch the demo to 7-22s, keep all 5 beats. Let the problem breathe before the product appears — hold the reveal until the viewer is actively looking for an answer.
- **Hook formula:** Problem-led. "If your [problem] looks like this, stop scrolling." Negative claim: "Most people use these wrong, here's the right way." Number-led: "5 moves with one band, beginner-friendly." Question-led: "What did they send this month?"
- **Creator performance:** Calm explainer tone — clear, step-by-step, confident but not corporate. Slightly more information-dense than casual UGC. Conversational tone delivers 31% higher engagement for DTC prospecting. Write for speech, not copy — short sentences, natural filler, occasional hesitation. Never script word-for-word across the entire video; lock hook + CTA, leave middle as beats. Energy level should match the product (calmer for sleep products, higher energy for fitness).
- **Product interaction:** Show the in-use moment — cable plugging in, case snapping on, product applied to skin, the pour. Use slow-motion for satisfying moments (connectors, pours, releases). Show "dirty → product applied → result" in one continuous clip where possible. Frame as medium shot, not extreme close-up. Wider framing reads as native UGC rather than product catalog clip.
- **Audio direction:** Conversational voiceover. Burn-in captions (bold sans-serif, 28px+, white text with black stroke, bottom 20% of screen, synced word-for-word with voiceover). Highlight key numbers and pain point words in contrast color. No music during speech.
- **CTA pattern:** Harder than other UGC modes — viewers have been educated, so the ask can be more direct. "Tap to shop the bundle." Include brand name or URL as non-negotiable. 74% of top-performing ads used a hard CTA. Problem-solution CTAs are strongest: name the exact problem in the same breath as the ask. CTA on screen for at least 3 seconds with high contrast text. One action, one destination.
- **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 vertical.
- **Duration sweet spot:** 15-30s. Core message must land within 15s. A tight 15s always outperforms a padded 30s. Edit pacing 30-50% faster than organic content: talking head shot 2-4s, product showcase 1.5-3s, before/after 2-3s each, CTA closing 3-5s.
- **Prompt opening:** `UGC how-to tutorial video shot on a phone in vertical 9:16, with handheld framing, real-environment context, a medium shot showing hand and product, step-by-step demonstration, and a 5-beat structure: hook, problem, demo, payoff, CTA.`
- **Mode seal:** `UGC how-to tutorial with real-environment staging, step-by-step product demonstration, conversational explainer delivery, burned-in captions, cut every 2-4 seconds, and a direct CTA including brand name.`
- **Relevant exclusions:** White sweep background, sterile studio set, over-scripted delivery, feature lists without demonstration. State positively: "real kitchen counter with soft morning light," "show the product working at the moment the voiceover names the feature."
- **Common failure modes:** Information overload (too many features/steps), unclear steps (viewer can't follow the sequence), too long (completion rate drops below 50%), starting with product name, feature-listing instead of demonstrating, no captions, corporate language, over-scripting, no single clear CTA, mentioning something not currently happening on screen.

## UGC Unboxing

- **ID:** `ugc-unboxing`
- **Aliases:** unboxing, reveal, first impression
- **Visual texture:** Phone-shot, 3 essential angles: overhead (top-down for the open and flat-lay look), 45-degree (roughly eye level if seated, catches face + product together), close-up macro (6-8 inches from product — money shot for texture, label, details). Natural light. Real surface (kitchen counter, desk, table). Tactile detail is the differentiator — packaging texture, product weight, how it feels to open.
- **Camera style:** 9:16 vertical. Highest resolution. Product and hands centered. Leave room at top for text overlays. Slow push-in on the reveal. Slight overhead tilt for the opening beat.
- **Lighting:** Natural light, handheld framing, and a real face talking to camera outperform color-graded brand footage. Polish is not the goal; clarity and authenticity are.
- **Narrative structure:** Anticipation → Revelation → Application. For 15s: hook (0-3s, sealed box visible), setup (3-8s, show the package, say what it is in one sentence), reveal (8-11s, open it, pull items out, react as you go), first impression (11-13s, hold product, show angles, honest take), CTA (13-15s, quick verdict + brand CTA). Reveal in beats, not all at once — tissue paper, the bottle, the card, each reveal is a beat with real reactions. Product visible by 0:03.
- **Hook formula:** Reaction hook (creator's face shows visible excitement before the box is open: "I've been waiting to try this"), contrast hook (open with the result, then cut back to the unboxing: show product in use, then "here's where it started"), social proof hook (borrow credibility before the reveal: "This thing has 4,000 five-star reviews — let's see if it's real").
- **Creator performance:** Genuine first reaction. Pre-reveal: anticipation, curiosity (eyes on the sealed box, slight lean-in, hands poised). Seal break: tension, focus (slow, deliberate motion, close-up on hands). Product emerges: surprise, delight (eyes widen, breath catch, genuine "oh" moment). Examination: satisfaction, appreciation (turning product in hands, feeling texture, tilting to light). Verdict: confidence, desire (holding product up to camera, direct address). The genuine first reaction sells desirability without a single claim.
- **Product interaction:** Reveal order matters — write from the buyer's point of view: lead with whatever detail makes someone want the product. Premium packaging, a satisfying texture, or a clever accessory often sells harder than the product itself. Handling must look natural and grounded — two-handed motion, box tapping, lifting, turning to camera. Show product in use after the unboxing. Don't just unbox and stop.
- **Audio direction:** ASMR-adjacent sensory detail: the sound of tape peeling, the texture of matte cardboard, the weight of the product, the click of a lid. Natural voice reactions. Captions burned in. Incorporate all five senses whenever possible to get mirror neurons firing.
- **CTA pattern:** Quick verdict + brand CTA or code. One action, one destination.
- **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 vertical.
- **Duration sweet spot:** 15-30s. Product visible by 0:03. TikTok: 9-21s. Always cut a 6s version of winners for cheap refreshes.
- **Prompt opening:** `UGC unboxing video shot on a phone in vertical 9:16, with three angles (overhead, 45-degree, close-up macro), natural lighting, a real surface, tactile detail, and a beat structure: hook, setup, reveal, first impression, CTA.`
- **Mode seal:** `UGC unboxing with phone-shot multi-angle coverage, natural lighting, layered reveal in beats, genuine first-reaction performance, sensory audio detail, and a quick-verdict CTA. Keep all printed text, logos, and packaging labels rigid and undeformed during handling.`
- **Relevant exclusions:** Over-produced aesthetic (glossy TV commercial look), perfectly lit product shots in a casual unboxing. State positively: "natural light, handheld framing, real surface," "genuine unscripted reactions."
- **Common failure modes:** Over-produced aesthetic breaks UGC trust, warped branding (logo/text bends during reveal — use image reference and inspect frames where the product turns), unnatural hands (hands that float or pass through the box), no hook (beautiful reveal with no opening hook gets scrolled past), showing the full product too early (kills the curiosity gap), burying the product (viewer can't tell what the product is in first few seconds), too long, sound-on assumption (no captions), one and done (shipping a single version wastes the format's advantage), boring packaging.

## Product Showcase

- **ID:** `product-showcase`
- **Aliases:** showcase, product hero, product film, product highlight
- **Visual texture:** Studio-quality, product-as-hero. No presenter. Clean, polished. Product fills 50%+ of frame. Product visible within first 2 seconds. End on the product, not the logo. Three to five distinct moments of the product in motion — even if motion is just a slow rotation under controlled light. One frame is the hero moment; two or three are supporting; everything else is rhythm.
- **Camera style:** Tripod or gimbal, 16:9 or 1:1. Low-angle hero (product towers slightly, looks grand), eye-level straight-on (pure objectivity), three-quarter 45° (most versatile, shows three planes), macro detail (proves quality — textures, stitching, materials), overhead (top surface, interior), symmetrical front (premium, luxury), abstract (extreme close-up of surface as art). One camera move per beat — if the camera rotates, do not also zoom and pan. Cap rotation to 10-15° to protect text/logos. "Object stays rigid: only camera moves." Lock exposure and white balance: single light direction, no time shift.
- **Lighting:** 45° hard key light to one side for defined shadows giving dimension and weight. Soft fill on the opposite side to retain detail. Never flat-light the hero shot. Macro/detail: raking light from a very shallow angle to reveal texture. Reflective surfaces (metal, glossy plastic, glass): diffusion tent environment or very large soft sources. Avoid overhead-only lighting. Surface choice communicates positioning: polished concrete/brushed metal/matte white for modern premium; wood/linen/unfinished stone for natural artisanal.
- **Narrative structure:** 1-2-1 for 6s (1 establishing → 2 detail/action → 1 brand identity). 4-beat for 15s (hero → detail → context → offer). 3-second framework: 0.0-0.8s hero close-up (product fills frame, instantly recognisable), 0.8-2.2s quick proof action (rotate, click, pour, stretch, open/close, before/after), 2.2-3.0s short on-screen label (3-6 words clarifying the benefit). 3-5 distinct moments of product in motion.
- **Hook formula:** Visual surprise — show the product doing something unexpected in the first frame. Hero close-up — product fills frame, instantly recognisable. Macro reveal — start with a macro detail and slowly pull back to reveal the full product.
- **Creator performance:** No presenter. The product speaks through motion, material, and light. Material storytelling: surfaces, textures, reflections, and transparency are characters. Close-up shots of materials tell a story about quality and craftsmanship that words cannot.
- **Product interaction:** Product is both the subject and the star. Camera movement, lighting, and transitions are choreographed around the product's form, materials, and details. Motion language should reflect the product's brand personality: precision for tech, fluidity for beauty, energy for sports.
- **Audio direction:** Typically no voiceover. Product speaks through motion and material. Design for sound-off, reward sound-on. Sensory grammar: micro-moments of 1-2 seconds that viewers remember — the sound of a fork through pastry, the sparkle of light catching a gemstone, the glug-and-foam of a quality pour. Subtle ambient motion sound (steam, refraction, sparkle) makes a still photo feel alive. Synchronized SFX for action moments: cap unscrewing, liquid pouring, button clicks. Music should match brand personality. Avoid stock-music gloss that screams "ad."
- **CTA pattern:** No spoken CTA. End on the product, not the logo. Visual space for price, claim, or CTA overlay. The final frame should make the viewer want the object on screen.
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 or 1:1 for product pages and autoplay loops. 9:16 for social feed placement.
- **Duration sweet spot:** 5-15s. 6-8s for single-beat autoplay loops. 10-15s for multi-beat product film. Anything longer starts to feel like a penalty for landing on the page.
- **Prompt opening:** `Studio-quality product showcase film in 16:9, with product-as-hero framing, controlled studio lighting, macro detail shots, slow rotation, and a 4-beat structure: hero, detail, context, offer.`
- **Mode seal:** `Product showcase with product-as-hero framing, one camera move per beat, 45-degree key light with soft fill, raking macro light for texture, locked exposure and white balance, minimalist background, and no voiceover. Protect 30% center rectangle from deformation. Keep all printed text and logos rigid, sharp, and undeformed.`
- **Relevant exclusions:** Cluttered backgrounds, presenter on camera, generic stock feel, logo intros, overhead-only lighting, single angle throughout. State positively: "clean minimal background with product as sole focal point," "eight to twelve distinct visual perspectives."
- **Common failure modes:** Cluttered backgrounds distract from product, weak product focus (presenter takes camera time), generic stock feel (stock-music gloss, soulless footage), opening with logo animation (lose a third of viewers in first 10s), overhead-only lighting (flat textures), too many features equally featured (spot feels flat — one hero, two-three supporting, rest is rhythm), no proof/result shown, beautiful but unclear (footage doesn't read as "this is a candle" — fails conversion test), too long (loses viewers at 20s), single angle throughout (brands that stand out use 8-12 distinct perspectives), text/logo deformation during rotation.

## Product Review

- **ID:** `product-review`
- **Aliases:** review, testimonial, opinion, creator review
- **Visual texture:** UGC-adjacent — real environments (kitchen, living room, backyard), unscripted talk, slight imperfections (shaky camera, natural lighting, imperfect framing). But more structured than pure UGC. Product in use, not on shelf. The "unpolished proof" phenomenon: unpolished content with a real customer achieves 2.1x higher CTR and 1.7x higher conversion than polished creator content.
- **Camera style:** 9:16 vertical. Handheld phone or casual tripod. Medium shot showing presenter and product. Capture B-roll: wide establishing, medium hand interactions, and macro details. Use consistent white balance so shots match. Show the product in use — unboxing, in-action, before/after, feature walk-through.
- **Lighting:** Real environments with available light. A little background noise is fine. Natural lighting signals "real person, not paid actor."
- **Narrative structure:** 5-beat spine. Hook (0-2s): pattern-interrupt and pre-qualify. Problem (2-7s): name the pain. Demo (7-22s): show the product working, proof not claim — longest beat. Payoff (22-27s): specific, measurable outcome. Soft CTA (27-30s): permission, not pressure. For 15s: compress demo to 8s, combine payoff + CTA into 3-4s. Lead with the verdict, not the introduction — share the headline take right away to reward viewers.
- **Hook formula:** Skeptic-to-convert ("I was skeptical, but my skin actually looks better"), timeframe-anchored ("Day 14 of this pre-workout, real review"), specific number ("I lost 14 pounds in 6 weeks"), social proof open ("I've been gatekeeping this for three months"), confession ("I've wanted this espresso machine for two years"). Pattern-interrupt and curiosity-gap hooks have the highest 3-second hold rate; social-proof opens have the highest CTR-to-click conversion among viewers who hold.
- **Creator performance:** Authentic opinion, not scripted endorsement. Real environments. "Ums" and "ahs" are part of the package — shows this is a real person. Balanced feedback — mention a minor learning curve or drawback to boost credibility. "I was skeptical too" mirrors the viewer's internal dialogue. Slight imperfections (shaky camera, imperfect framing, natural lighting) signal "real person, not paid actor." Brighter videos, higher visual dynamics, reviewer face visible, more positive facial emotions, lower voice pitch, and faster speech rates are all associated with higher perceived helpfulness.
- **Product interaction:** Show, don't tell — a hand actually using the product beats a voiceover claiming it works. Demonstration types: unboxing (packaging, what's included, first impression), product in use (solving a real problem in real time), before-and-after (ultimate convincer for skincare, cleaning, home organization), feature walk-through (one clearly identifiable feature demonstrated). Name the specific on-screen action that proves the claim — pour, apply, fold, click, before/after toggle. Product fills 50%+ of frame. The best reviews demonstrate the product in action, rather than just featuring a customer talking to camera with the product sitting on a shelf.
- **Audio direction:** Voice is mandatory — silent videos engaged at 0.90x vs 1.09x with voice. Voice won in every single category tested. "Voice" does not have to be a scripted studio VO — a founder talking to camera, a creator narrating as they unbox, or a quick spoken line over B-roll all count. Natural voice with room echo. No music during speech.
- **CTA pattern:** Single action, single destination. Soft not pushy — feels like a recommendation from a friend. "If you've tried everything and nothing's worked, just try this one." Map CTA to the metric being billed against. Last 2-3 seconds. Never end on a fade-to-logo with no instruction — that's where conversions leak out. Testimonial CTA: "If you've tried everything and nothing's worked, just try this one." Skeptic-to-convert CTA: "I got mine from [website], there's a discount code: [CODE]."
- **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 vertical.
- **Duration sweet spot:** 15-30s. TikTok: 9-21s (best 15-25s). Reels: 15-30s (sweet spot 20s). Meta Feed: 15-30s. YouTube Shorts: 15-35s. Quick-clips under 7s are the worst duration (0.76x engagement). Medium videos outperform (1.17x). Hook by 1.5-2.0s. Change something every 3-5 seconds: new angle, graphic, or demo.
- **Prompt opening:** `UGC product review video shot on a phone in vertical 9:16, with real-environment setting, natural lighting, handheld framing, hands-on product demonstration, authentic creator delivery, and a 5-beat structure: hook, problem, demo, payoff, soft CTA.`
- **Mode seal:** `Product review with real-environment texture, hands-on product demonstration, authentic unscripted delivery, one honest limitation alongside the wins, natural voice with room sound, and a single soft CTA. Preserve all printed text and product labels rigid and undeformed.`
- **Relevant exclusions:** Overly polished production (reads as commercial), product on shelf with no demonstration, generic claims without specifics, multiple CTAs, cinematic production style. State positively: "real kitchen with available light," "show the product actively being used during the review."
- **Common failure modes:** Overly positive with no cons (zero downsides persuades no one — reads as fake), no real testing (talking head with product on shelf provides no visual proof), too polished/scripted (reads as paid advertisement), generic claims ("It works great!" is meaningless), no hook in first 2s, too short under 7s (worst duration), spec dumps without context, multiple CTAs (dilutes the click), no demographic matching (22-year-old presenting a product for 50-year-olds), cinematic production (0.64x engagement — worst production style), silent video with no voice (0.90x), ending on logo fade with no CTA.

## TV Spot

- **ID:** `tv-spot`
- **Aliases:** broadcast, commercial, tv ad, broadcast spot
- **Visual texture:** Broadcast-grade. Professional cinematography. Multi-shot, dynamic cutting. Color-graded to unified look across all shots. Rec. 709 color space for broadcast. Title-safe framing — all text and important info in central 80-90% of screen. Single, sharply defined idea — boil the message down to one clear hook graspable within the first 5 seconds.
- **Camera style:** 16:9, professional rig. 4-8 distinct scenes for 30s, each running 3-8s. Fewer than 4 feels slow; more than 8 feels frantic. Build a small visual grammar: if brand needs to feel precise, camera movement is restrained and deliberate; if story needs energy, camera moves more freely. Give every visual choice a job — a shallow depth of field, gimbal move, or dramatic light source does not automatically make an image cinematic. Those are tools that become direction when they have a reason to be there.
- **Lighting:** Professional, controlled. Cohesive set and wardrobe design. Existing practical light can be part of the visual identity — control what matters and remove what distracts, not erase the truth of the place. Color grade for unified look across all shots. TVs boost contrast and saturation, so send a mellower grade. Check on actual TV screens.
- **Narrative structure:** Choose one framework. PAS (Problem 0-10s → Agitate 10-20s → Solve 20-30s) for direct response, pain-point products. BAB (Before 0-10s → After 10-20s → Bridge 20-30s) for aspirational brands, transformation products. HSO (Tease 0-3s → Build 3-15s → Payoff 15-30s) for social, DTC, curiosity-driven. The 0-5-22-30 structure: hook (0-5s, 10-15 words), message (5-22s, 40-50 words: 6-7s on problem, 6-7s introducing solution, 4-5s on proof), CTA (22-30s, 10-15 words). Do not save the climax for the end — most viewers are gone by then. Start strong with both emotion and branding, maintain momentum with several emotional peaks, intersperse brand cues throughout.
- **Hook formula:** Start mid-scene in a relatable situation. Emotion first, not information — joy, hope, surprise, or nostalgia. Create recognition ("that's exactly my life"), curiosity ("I don't know where this is going"), or surprise ("I wasn't expecting that"). Build tension through specificity — vague stories feel manufactured, specific stories feel true. Include numbers, timeframes, and sensory details. Use the Zeigarnik effect: an unresolved conflict creates an information gap that allocates attention until resolved.
- **Creator performance:** Professional delivery. Testimonial format outperforms announcer-voice. Scripts that read like a real person talking about a real problem sound like testimonials because they are structured like them. "No voiceover announcer voice, no dramatic music." Corporate language in a personal story breaks the narrative spell. "We believe in empowering individuals" is corporate speak. "She was tired of waking up exhausted" is a story. The viewer is the protagonist; the brand is the guide (Gandalf, not Frodo). Make the viewer identify with the hero and emotionally experience the transformation.
- **Product interaction:** Product takes center frame in beautiful lighting, with owner or narration as supporting context. Product on white background fails on TV — the viewer needs to see the product in context, in use, in a moment that makes them want it. Sensory grammar: color, texture, sound, the imagined taste or feel. These 1-2 second micro-moments are what viewers remember. The product enters the story as the answer to the tension built — not as a pitch, as a resolution. "That's when I found [product]" works because the viewer has been waiting for the resolution. End on the emotional payoff, not the product — the viewer wants what the product makes possible.
- **Audio direction:** Professional voiceover or testimonial-style delivery. Licensed music bed — purposeful music and sound design reinforce the story and keep viewers immersed. Sound effects that fit the tone. Broadcast mixes should be brighter than web mixes because TV speakers mute highs and make audio sound muddier. Audio loudness normalization: -24 LKFS (CALM Act, ±2 LU) for broadcast; -14 LKFS (EBU R128) for CTV/streaming. Closed captions required: CEA-608/708 for broadcast; SRT/TTML for CTV/digital.
- **CTA pattern:** Brand lock-in in final 3-5 seconds with name, URL, and visual identity. Spoken CTA with on-screen text. Specific, simple, memorable. "Tap the link, use code X for free shipping on your first order." Weak CTA: "Check us out." Strong CTA: "Tap the link, use code X for free shipping on your first order."
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 for broadcast and CTV. 9:16, 1:1, 4:5 acceptable for digital-only placement.
- **Duration sweet spot:** 15-30s. 30s is standard for broadcast. CTV is broadcast, not digital — creative requirements are broadcast standards (30s spots, professional production values, broadcast-quality audio), not 15s vertical video.
- **Prompt opening:** `Broadcast-grade TV spot in 16:9, with professional cinematography, multi-shot dynamic cutting, cohesive color grading, [PAS/BAB/HSO] narrative framework, multiple emotional peaks, and a 0-5-22-30 structure: hook, message, CTA.`
- **Mode seal:** `TV spot with broadcast-grade cinematography, [framework] narrative structure, viewer-as-protagonist direction, product-in-context hero shot, multiple emotional peaks with interspersed brand cues, professional voiceover, licensed music bed, and brand lock-in in the final 3-5 seconds.`
- **Relevant exclusions:** Product on white background, slow-burn narrative on YouTube, corporate language in personal story, single grand finale at the end. State positively: "product in context, in use, in a moment that makes the viewer want it," "start strong with both emotion and branding."
- **Common failure modes:** Overstuffed narrative (three value props in 30s = zero memorable value props), weak hook (three seconds to earn the next 27), poor brand recall (brand not in first 3s for pre-roll), product reveal too early (viewer reclassifies from story to ad), corporate language in personal story, no specific antagonist (story has no tension), skipping the transformation (ending on product instead of outcome), leading with product not problem, burying the hook (first 5s on company name), vague social proof ("thousands" vs "47,812 five-star reviews"), weak CTA ("Check us out"), too many claims, slow-burn narrative on YouTube, under-specifying the brief, over-optimizing for production values at expense of idea quality, cutting post-production time.

## Wild Card

- **ID:** `wild-card`

## UGC Virtual Try-On

- **ID:** `ugc-virtual-try-on`
- **Aliases:** try-on ugc, fitting ugc, casual try-on, outfit try-on
- **Visual texture:** Phone-shot UGC. Real environments (bedroom, closet, dressing room, car, sidewalk, coffee shop) perform better than studio sets because they read as authentic. Deliberately unpolished — authenticity is the signal that makes it perform (6.2x ROAS documented vs 0.8x for polished product-only). Do not color-grade to a brand palette, do not overlay logos, do not add consistent typography across shots — polish breaks the authenticity illusion. TikTok's algorithm punishes content that looks like an ad and rewards content that looks like a person.
- **Camera style:** 9:16 vertical. Camera lens at waist level (belly button height) — the golden rule that ensures zero perspective distortion. Phone strictly perpendicular to the floor. Top-down shooting enlarges head/shoulders 15-20% and shortens legs. Bottom-up creates massive chin and heavy hips. 2-2.5m distance — closer than 1.5m causes wide-angle distortion. Frame from mid-thigh to just above the head. Multi-angle coverage: front, back, 3/4 left, 3/4 right, side profile. Walk-around loop: model walks slowly while camera circles 180-360°. Detail close-ups: neckline, cuff, hem, fabric texture, zipper pull, stitching. Movement passes: turn (full 360°), walk (shows drape, hem swing), sit/stand (demonstrates rise and waist comfort), arm movement (sleeve fit), fabric stretch test (for knits), backlit transparency pass (for sheer fabrics).
- **Lighting:** Frontal natural daylight — face the window so light hits evenly from the front. Smooths skin tone, gives clear image. Window + white reflector diffuses light naturally. CRI above 90 for accurate fabric and skin tones. 3000-4000K warm white. Light from a single defined direction creates depth and makes fabric look three-dimensional. Avoid: harsh overhead lighting (dark circles under eyes, obscures waistline), backlighting (silhouettes the subject), uneven lighting (exaggerates seam lines on phone screens).
- **Narrative structure:** Hook (0-2s, bold before/after clip or clear visual promise: "Watch how this dress fits on different body types") → Transformation (show the garment going on — from holding it up to wearing it) → Multiple angles (quick cuts of walking, sitting, close fabric) → Styling moment (one piece styled dressed up or down) → CTA. Try-on transformation drives 18-32% higher add-to-cart rates for fashion.
- **Hook formula:** Before/after visual promise ("Watch how this dress fits on different body types"), skeptic-to-convert ("I was skeptical about the fit but..."), styling promise ("One piece styled three ways"), GRWM context ("Getting ready for a date in this new dress").
- **Creator performance:** Spontaneous reactions. Initial reaction to seeing/touching the fabric (unboxing moment). First reaction while putting it on (surprise, delight, or honest assessment). Movement reaction (how it feels to walk, sit, move in it). Final verdict (would they actually wear it, when, and why). One honest limitation alongside the wins: "The material is thinner than expected but perfect for summer" or "Runs slightly large — size down if between sizes." Specificity beats generality: "The ruching hides everything" beats "flattering silhouette."
- **Product interaction:** Show fit, drape, movement. Walk to show hem swing and fabric flow. Stretch test for knits (gently pull to show elasticity and recovery). How light catches the fabric (especially for silk, satin, chiffon). Quick cuts on stitching, fabric weave, zipper pull. State the model's height and usual size explicitly. Note whether the piece runs large, small, or true to size. Include at least two body types or sizes in a single ad to increase perceived fit relevance. One piece styled three ways (dressed up, dressed down, layered) sells versatility and raises AOV.
- **Audio direction:** Conversational voiceover. Trending sounds. Natural room sound. No music during speech.
- **CTA pattern:** "Tap to see sizes" or "Limited stock — add to cart." Direct but not aggressive.
- **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 vertical.
- **Duration sweet spot:** 9-21s (TikTok), 15-30s (Reels).
- **Prompt opening:** `UGC virtual try-on video shot on a phone in vertical 9:16, with waist-level camera, real-environment setting, natural daylight, full-body multi-angle coverage, movement passes, and a transformation structure: hook, garment-on, multiple angles, styling, CTA.`
- **Mode seal:** `UGC virtual try-on with waist-level phone camera, natural frontal daylight, real-environment background, full-body multi-angle coverage with movement passes, spontaneous creator reactions, one honest fit note, and no color grading or logo overlays. Preserve all printed text and garment labels rigid and undeformed.`
- **Relevant exclusions:** Studio sets, polished color grading, logo overlays, consistent typography across shots, top-down camera angle, all-black clothing, crossed arms. State positively: "real bedroom or dressing room with natural window light," "waist-level camera, phone perpendicular to floor."
- **Common failure modes:** Top-down camera angle (enlarges head/shoulders 15-20%, shortens legs), slouching or half-turned poses (obscures anatomical markers), crossed arms or hands on waist (blocks chest and stomach area), all-black clothing (absorbs light, kills shadows), pure white clothing (overexposes, blends with wall), harsh overhead lighting (unflattering shadows), backlighting (silhouettes the subject), over-polishing UGC content (breaks authenticity illusion — do not color-grade, overlay logos, or add typography), scripted creator reactions (reads as brand-produced), only showing one body type (leaves most potential buyers unanswered), no movement (doesn't show drape, hem swing, fabric behavior), cluttered/busy background, shooting too wide (face preservation fails when face occupies a small fraction), asking AI to both preserve garment and create editorial look in one step (split into stages: standardize product first, then push style).

## Pro Virtual Try-On

- **ID:** `virtual-try-on`
- **Aliases:** try-on pro, fitting pro, editorial try-on, model-driven try-on
- **Visual texture:** Polished, model-driven, editorial quality. Controlled studio or curated location. Color-graded. Professional production value. Higher brand equity, lower direct-response ROAS than UGC. Best for brand positioning, premium products, editorial campaigns.
- **Camera style:** Tripod or gimbal, 85-135mm equivalent lens for compression (separates product from background for hero aspect). Same multi-angle coverage as UGC (front, back, 3/4 left, 3/4 right, side profile, walk-around loop, detail macros) but with studio discipline. Full-body, centered, professionally composed.
- **Lighting:** Soft 3-point studio. Key: large softbox at 45° above camera height. Fill: reflector or soft light opposite key. Backlight: hair/rim light to separate model from background. CRI above 90. 3000-4000K. Single directional source for fabric depth. Cross-polarized for shiny embellishments or jewelry. Backlit transparency for sheer fabrics.
- **Narrative structure:** Hero → Detail → Context → Offer (4-beat). More aspirational than problem-solution. Hero (product on model in full glory) → Detail (macro on fabric, fit, construction) → Context (product in lifestyle setting or styled look) → Offer (product framed with space for price or CTA overlay).
- **Hook formula:** Hero visual — product on model in beautiful lighting, full-body, editorial framing. Aspirational opening — show the lifestyle moment the product enables. Slow reveal — start with a detail macro and pull back to reveal the full look.
- **Creator performance:** Model or trained presenter. Polished delivery. Aspirational + conversational — inspires without sales pressure. Identity-driven. Professional posing but natural movement during the walk and turn passes.
- **Product interaction:** Same fit, drape, and movement demonstrations as UGC but with editorial quality. Steam garments before shooting. Show the full garment — no cropping at sleeves, hem, or collar. One product per clip in the primary try-on. Garment preparation: steamed, clean, full garment visible.
- **Audio direction:** Professional voiceover. Licensed music. Sound design with SFX (fabric rustling, footsteps). Music should match the brand's editorial tone.
- **CTA pattern:** Brand-forward. "Shop the collection." More aspirational than direct-response.
- **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 for social, 16:9 or 1:1 for editorial and product pages.
- **Duration sweet spot:** 20-30s. Longer for editorial.
- **Prompt opening:** `Editorial virtual try-on video in 9:16, with professional model, controlled studio lighting, 85mm compression, full-body multi-angle coverage, movement passes, color-graded, and a 4-beat structure: hero, detail, context, offer.`
- **Mode seal:** `Pro virtual try-on with studio 3-point lighting, 85mm compression lens, full-body multi-angle coverage with movement passes, professional model delivery, editorial color grade, licensed music, and brand-forward CTA. Preserve all printed text and garment labels rigid and undeformed.`
- **Relevant exclusions:** Phone-shot texture, handheld micro-shake, real-environment backgrounds (these belong in UGC try-on). State positively: "controlled studio with seamless backdrop," "tripod-mounted camera with 85mm prime lens."
- **Common failure modes:** Same camera mistakes as UGC (top-down, crossed arms, all-black clothing, backlighting). The primary failure is using UGC texture here or vice versa — this mode is intentionally polished. Over-polishing UGC content breaks the authenticity illusion; using studio polish here is the correct choice. Other failures: slouching, no movement, single angle, cluttered background, shooting too wide.
