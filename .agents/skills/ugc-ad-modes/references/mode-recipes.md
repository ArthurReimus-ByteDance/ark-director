# UGC Ad Mode Recipes

Read the requested mode only. These are **creative starting points**, not API
requirements or performance evidence. User instructions and supported product
facts govern every recipe. Example dialogue describes hypothetical concepts;
use it only when its product operation is supported. Frame-specific dimensions,
cut rates, lens choices, and durations remain adjustable to the actual action.

Across all modes, exact copy belongs to approved references or finishing. A
synthetic demonstration cannot substantiate efficacy, comfort, sizing, savings,
or personal experience. Do not fabricate a con, testimonial, offer, or review
count. Identity and product fidelity survive changes in presentation style.

## UGC

- **Texture and camera:** Casual phone-shot in a real environment; eye-level
  medium framing gives the viewer both presenter and product context. Controlled
  exposure and a steady phone are compatible with casual content.
- **Light:** A motivated window or practical source that keeps the action clear;
  natural light has no assumed algorithmic ranking advantage.
- **Story:** Start with a relevant action or question; show one supported feature;
  return to the viewer's decision. Hook/problem/demo/payoff/CTA is a useful longer
  spine, not a required five-shot template.
- **Angles:** Demonstrate a known latch; address a supplied storage constraint;
  reveal a documented modular part. Use a genuine experience only if supplied.
- **Performance/audio:** Conversational direct address, pause for the action,
  then resume. Native room sound; music optional if speech remains intelligible.
- **CTA:** One supplied action, usually conversational. No invented link or urgency.
- **Watch for:** Fake usage timelines, forced fillers, generic praise, too many
  benefits, or product behavior unsupported by the supplied information.

## UGC How-To

- **Texture/camera:** Real use context with hands, object, and destination all
  visible. Move closer only when the mechanism needs detail; never cut away from
  a necessary step merely to satisfy a fixed edit rhythm.
- **Story:** Goal → required setup → intelligible steps → observable result → CTA.
  Use the actual instructions supplied. If essential operating steps are absent,
  ask for them or draft a non-operational feature overview instead of guessing.
- **Performance/audio:** Clear short instructions aligned with the visible action.
  Leave pauses long enough to follow the sequence. Exact user copy stays intact.
- **CTA:** Requested action appropriate to the viewer's next step; brand and
  destination must be supplied. Caption styling is a finishing decision.
- **Watch for:** Invented operation, hand/object occlusion, impossible step count,
  voice naming a feature that is not on screen, or a result implying unsupported efficacy.

## UGC Unboxing

- **Texture/camera:** A real surface; overhead for opening, an oblique angle for
  presenter and box, detail view for a relevant component. Use requested coverage
  without cramming every angle into an impossibly short reveal.
- **Story:** Identify the product/category, reveal the package layers that actually
  exist, examine a supplied detail, and show a supported use or closing view.
- **Performance/audio:** Curious attention to the package, deliberate handling,
  and sensory tape/cardboard/lid sounds. Scripted performance is not evidence of
  a genuine first-use endorsement. Neutral discovery works without personal history.
- **CTA:** Supplied destination or a clearly proposed next step.
- **Watch for:** Invented accessories, review totals, staged before/after efficacy,
  malformed hands, and packaging/text drift during rotation.

## Product Showcase

- **Texture/camera:** Product-as-hero, normally without a presenter. Controlled
  framing, static or deliberate movement; choose macro, profile, or overhead to
  reveal the actual differentiating feature. Reflective materials may benefit
  from broad diffused light; textured surfaces may benefit from raking light.
- **Story:** Hero → detail/action → closing product state. If the user requests
  1-2-1, use one establishing beat, two detail/action beats, one closing beat.
  Keep actions simple enough for the runtime; a single meaningful reveal can work.
- **Audio:** Tactile sound or a restrained bed when requested; silence is valid.
- **CTA:** Honor a requested spoken CTA; otherwise a product closing view with
  separately planned text can serve a product-page or brand placement.
- **Watch for:** Decorative motion obscuring the mechanism, text distortion,
  generic "premium" language without material direction, or impossible benefits.

## Product Review

- **Texture/camera:** Casual presenter or a product comparison in real context.
  Frame the assessed detail clearly. A silent review is valid when the user wants
  visible comparison and finishing text instead of dialogue.
- **Story:** Question → supplied evidence or observable feature → bounded verdict.
  Skeptic-to-convert is optional and requires actual experience to support a
  first-person story. An unsupported con stays unresolved rather than invented.
- **Performance/audio:** Direct, specific explanation. Preserve real quoted words
  and their scope; do not exaggerate skepticism or claim satisfaction not supplied.
- **CTA:** One requested action, fitting the information the review established.
- **Watch for:** Synthetic use passed off as real experience, efficacy inferred from
  imagery, mandatory praise/criticism balance, and unsupported superlatives.

## TV Spot

- **Texture/camera:** A coherent polished visual grammar for the actual placement.
  Precision may call for still frames; energy may call for motion. Aspect ratio,
  duration, color, caption, and loudness delivery requirements come from the brief
  or verified destination specification, not one universal broadcast preset.
- **Story:** One clear idea; use requested PAS, before/after/bridge, or other
  structure. Transformation imagery must not promise unsupported product results.
  Multiple emotional peaks are optional unless requested; brand timing serves
  the stated brief and story rather than a presumed platform rule.
- **Performance/audio:** Professional narration, dialogue, or evidenced testimonial.
  Music and SFX support the idea, with a usable music source when specified.
- **CTA:** Exact supplied brand/action/destination. Plan title copy in finishing.
- **Watch for:** Invented discounts, testimonial histories, conversion statistics,
  excessive scene changes, or a polished look with no intelligible proposition.

## Wild Card

- **Texture/camera:** State the dominant visual logic and one purposeful departure
  such as reverse chronology, scale play, or an unexpected reveal.
- **Story:** Make the deviation clarify the product idea, not merely decorate it.
  A clear setup and payoff can replace the standard five-beat spine.
- **Performance/audio:** Match the chosen viewpoint; protect the product's real
  properties even in surreal staging. Mark fantasy actions as conceptual rather
  than implying actual product capability.
- **CTA:** The supplied action; do not add a joke offer as if commercially valid.
- **Watch for:** Novelty without a proposition, conflicting textures, and fantasy
  turning into an unsubstantiated performance claim.

## UGC Virtual Try-On

- **Texture/camera:** Casual real-room presentation. Choose level framing and
  enough distance to show the requested garment and movement without misleading
  perspective. Waist-level is one useful full-body option, not a zero-distortion
  guarantee. A detail view may need a different setup.
- **Story:** Establish garment → turn/walk to show design → relevant detail or
  styling option → closing action. Use only movements that fit the runtime.
- **Performance/audio:** Neutral observation or a supplied actual fit account.
  Height, worn size, size recommendations, comfort, and "runs large" claims must
  be provided; model-generated appearance cannot establish those facts.
- **CTA:** Requested action such as viewing available sizes when that destination
  is supplied. Do not invent limited-stock pressure.
- **Watch for:** Garment identity drift, hidden hems, invented body measurements,
  unsupported fit advice, or claiming one rendered body predicts every buyer's fit.

## Pro Virtual Try-On

- **Texture/camera:** Editorial garment presentation in a studio or curated location.
  Choose distance, perspective, and coverage to retain the full requested garment
  plus meaningful detail. A longer lens is an option, not a mandatory numeric range.
- **Light:** Controlled sources show fabric behavior and silhouette; light direction
  depends on the actual material, not a compulsory three-light layout.
- **Story:** Hero → construction detail → movement/context → closing state.
  Preserve the supplied garment, model identity, and approved treatment.
- **Audio/CTA:** Requested sound and brand action. Silent editorial is valid.
- **Watch for:** Style changes redesigning the garment, unsupported comfort/fit
  inference, decorative posing hiding construction, or a detail shot without context.

## Custom recipe template

Specify the visual texture, relevant audience question, supported product truth,
opening event, demonstration/payoff, camera/light purpose, audio, and requested
CTA. Name the likely failure and a visible acceptance criterion. If alternatives
are requested, vary the persuasive mechanism while keeping facts and locks fixed.
