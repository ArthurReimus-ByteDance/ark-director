# Vocabulary

Read [the entrypoint](../SKILL.md) for native-audio and wording-preservation rules.

## Vocabulary simplification

**Simplification is an optional editorial remedy, not a preprocessing step.**
Use it when the user has authorized wording changes and the actual problem is
wordiness, unsuitable register, or a pronunciation issue for which an agreed
alternative preserves the intended line. Familiarity, length, and formality are
not measured predictions of model failure.

## Choose the remedy from evidence

| Evidence | Next step |
| --- | --- |
| Exact approved line; no observed defect | Keep it and direct the delivery |
| Correct words but stiff rhythm | Adjust phrasing, pauses, emphasis, or tactic outside the spoken text |
| A recording mispronounces a particular word | Verify the intended reading; test one targeted note |
| User asks for more conversational wording | Offer a register-preserving alternative and show the delta |
| Period, literary, ceremonial, or character-specific speech | Preserve the chosen register; do not modernize by default |
| Names, titles, product copy, quotes, or locked phrases | Preserve exact words; resolve pronunciation with evidence |
| No linguistic or listening evidence | State a hypothesis or ask for a reading; do not claim a word is wrong |

## Minimal editorial procedure

1. Quote the existing line and identify the concrete issue.
2. Name the facts to preserve: who acts on whom, negation, tense/aspect,
   degree of certainty, intention, relationship, politeness, and tone.
3. Suggest the smallest revision that fixes that issue, with an explanation.
4. Keep the original canonical until the revision is accepted or the user has
   explicitly authorized making that kind of edit. Do not infer acceptance
   merely because the alternative was displayed.
5. Confirm the revised line still fits its dramatic beat. A shorter line can
   change commitment, accusation, or social distance even when its topic matches.

Word length alone does not mandate sentence splitting. Contractions and English
substitutions are stylistic choices; do not make every speaker sound like the
same contemporary Manila speaker.

## Applying an agreed change to separate audio

If the user explicitly requested a separate dialogue track, use the same
accepted line in both audio and video prompts. Editing either prompt changes
the shared wording contract and requires corresponding review. It does not
authorize replacing an existing audio take or submitting another one silently.

See [hypothetical dialogue repairs](dialogue-repairs.md) for a worked editorial
example. Its before/after lines are invented illustrations, not approved project
text or evidence of a successful model generation.
