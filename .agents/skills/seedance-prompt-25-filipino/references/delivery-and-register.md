# Delivery and Register

Read [the entrypoint](../SKILL.md). This reference supports creative delivery;
it is not a universal description of how every Filipino speaker talks.

## Intonation direction

Direct the **utterance in context**. Establish who is speaking to whom, what
response they seek, what they can reveal, and what changes during the line.
Use a few observable cues: a pause before a request, a quieter correction,
a held ending while awaiting an answer, or firmer emphasis on an agreed word.
Do not prescribe one flat melody or make every question rise identically.

The [Farinella and Jun study](https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/PLSA/article/view/5966)
reports variable tonal alignment in Tagalog. Its linguistic analysis supports
context-sensitive review; it does not supply a universal prompt recipe or
prove how a generation model will respond.

## Naturalness diagnostic

| Question | Repair only if the scene supports it |
| --- | --- |
| Does the speaker know what they want from the listener? | Give the line a concrete immediate intent |
| Is the delivery explaining facts the listener already knows? | Shift emphasis toward the request or subtext; offer wording changes separately |
| Does the emotional label contradict the action? | Choose playable behavior consistent with the character's tactic |
| Is the wording intentionally formal, literary, comic, or awkward? | Preserve the effect; naturalness is relative to that character |
| Is the line spoken to an elder, peer, customer, stranger, or close family? | Keep the established relationship and approved politeness choices |
| Is a recorded performance available? | Compare rhythm and emphasis with the approved reference rather than a stereotype |

## Taglish code-switching

Keep the user's approved mix of languages. Code-switching varies with speaker,
setting, relationship, and intended effect. Do not apply blanket vowel, stress,
consonant-substitution, or accent rules to every English word. Do not introduce
extra English words, contractions, or discourse particles solely because the
scene is urban or the speaker is young.

```text
Dialogue language: Taglish, <approved register/variety>.
Preserve the exact language switches in the supplied line and the speaker's
established voice. <Speaker> says: {<exact approved words>}
Delivery: <a concrete scene-specific cue>.
```

## Speech registers and politeness

Politeness particles, contractions, word choice, and address terms can change
the audience's read of the relationship. Preserve approved `po`, `opo`, `ho`,
`oho`, address terms, and their placement unless rewriting them is authorized.
Their presence or absence is not by itself proof of disrespect, sarcasm, or
wrong grammar. Do not convert every casual `oo po` into `opo` automatically.

Ask or state an uncertainty when the intended relationship is missing. A
formal line may be right for a character trying to create distance, and a
suddenly polite line between peers may be a deliberate tactic. Ground the
judgment in the scene rather than a generic age-based rule.

## Minimal direction examples — HYPOTHETICAL

These are invented acting directions, not approved project lines or measured
model outcomes:

```text
A daughter asks her father for permission. She holds the request until he
looks up, then says the approved line quietly and waits for his response.
```

```text
Two coworkers disagree privately. The speaker keeps the approved Taglish line,
places emphasis on the requested deadline, and lets the final phrase hang
until the colleague answers.
```

Use [hypothetical dialogue repairs](dialogue-repairs.md) only when diagnosing
naturalness or comparing an authorized rewrite with a delivery-only repair.
