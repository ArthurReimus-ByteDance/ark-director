# Hypothetical Causal Repairs

These cases are **HYPOTHETICAL**, invented to demonstrate audit method. They
are not source screenplay evidence, user approvals, or measured audience
responses. Read [the skill](../SKILL.md) for the unchanged five definitions.

## Case 1 — unclear geography, not a missing story obstacle

Supplied hypothetical context: Mina must hand in a signed form before the office
closes. The clerk is behind a glass door and can still accept it. The excerpt is:

```text
INT. OFFICE LOBBY - EVENING

The clerk turns the key. Mina hurries over and holds out the form.
```

**Established:** a deadline, required handoff, clerk, glass door, key turn, and
Mina's approach. **Missing:** Mina's position relative to the door and whether
the handoff is physically possible after the key turns. **Inferred:** her
approach is a tactic to complete the established delivery goal.

Do not invent another exit, a helpful guard, or an earlier promise. Ask whether
the intended beat is a last-second handoff or a failed approach. The distinction
changes the outcome and cannot be silently decided by a staging note.

**HYPOTHETICAL minimal repair if the author intends a failed approach:**

```text
INT. OFFICE LOBBY - EVENING

Mina is two steps outside the glass door when the clerk turns the key.
She reaches the handle. It will not turn. She raises the signed form
against the glass where the clerk can see it.
```

Causal consequence: the failed handle test supplies information that the door
is locked; the visible form becomes a new tactic to gain the clerk's attention.
The goal, deadline, existing objects, and clerk remain unchanged. The exact
starting distance is proposed staging, not a fact recovered from the original.
Whether this is a reversal depends on the enclosing sequence and audience read;
this excerpt alone does not establish either. Do not manufacture a value shift.

## Case 2 — natural dialogue without an invented motive

Supplied hypothetical context: Arun seeks the clerk's attention after finding
the door locked. The line is locked:

```text
ARUN
Please look at the form.
```

The author reports that the beat feels like exposition. **Established:** a
request for attention and a visible form. **Inferred:** Arun hopes attention
will reopen the handoff opportunity. **Missing:** the clerk's response and the
resolved sequence; do not declare a reversal or audience-value shift yet.

**HYPOTHETICAL minimal repair, keeping the line:**

```text
Arun raises the form into the clerk's eyeline and waits. The clerk
keeps walking. Arun taps once against the glass.

ARUN
Please look at the form.
```

The offered clerk movement is a proposed beat, not supplied evidence. It makes
the escalation from showing to tapping playable without rewriting the words or
inventing a family relationship. If that response conflicts with the author's
intent, retain the original and resolve the response instead.

Acceptance: geography makes the gesture readable; the tactic follows the
information Arun has; exact dialogue survives; the later sequence remains
available for the author's existing reversal and value shift.

## Case 3 — an incomplete excerpt is not a failed resolved sequence

An excerpt ends immediately after the hero learns the first route is blocked.
The next scene is unavailable. Record the obstacle opening and returned
information, then mark the sequence endpoint and reversal/value-shift trajectory
as **missing**. Review whether the present tactic is reasonable on the available
knowledge. Do not insert a surprise betrayal merely to fill all five labels.
