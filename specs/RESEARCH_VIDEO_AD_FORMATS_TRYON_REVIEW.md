## 1. Virtual Try-On (UGC Organic & Polished)

### Core Format Definition

A virtual try-on video shows a person wearing a garment or accessory, demonstrating
how it fits, moves, and styles on a real body. It exists on a spectrum from
**UGC organic** (phone-shot, casual, authentic) to **polished/studio** (controlled
lighting, professional framing, editorial quality). The format's primary job is to
answer three questions a shopper silently asks: *Will it fit me? Will it look like
the photo? Will I actually wear it?* ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce))

### 1.1 Staging: How to Present Clothing/Accessories on a Person

**Garment preparation:**
- Steam garments before shooting to remove wrinkles that read as poor quality on camera ([Benly](https://benly.ai/learn/tiktok-ads/tiktok-ads-fashion-brands))
- Show the **full garment** — no cropping at sleeves, hem, or collar. The model
  needs to see the complete garment to assess fit accurately ([Vensa](https://vensa.app/best-practices))
- **One product per image/clip** in the primary try-on. Accessories and layered
  outfits confuse the viewer and dilute the fit message. Style suggestions go in
  a secondary styling clip ([Vensa](https://vensa.app/best-practices), [Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f))

**Pose fundamentals (for AI generation):**
- Stand straight, shoulders squared, weight evenly distributed, looking straight
  ahead. The model needs symmetry to correctly determine figure proportions
  ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2))
- Arms slightly away from hips (10–15 cm gap) — visible space between arms and
  waist so the garment's silhouette at the waist is readable ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2))
- Feet shoulder-width apart or slightly narrower — the model needs to see the
  space between legs to correctly construct inseam ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2))
- **Do not** cross arms over chest or place hands on waist — this blocks the
  décolleté and stomach area ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2))
- Standardized pose set: neutral front (arms relaxed), neutral back (shoulders
  squared), 3/4 left & right (reveals waist shaping and flare), side profile
  (essential for trousers' rise and front volume) ([Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f))

**Background:**
- Plain, neutral-colored backdrop that doesn't distract from the product
  ([Alsett](https://alsett.com/creating-high-quality-try-on-videos-expert-guide/))
- Solid color — don't blend in with the wall. If wall is white, wear gray/beige
  base layer; if wallpaper is dark, choose light gray ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2))
- For UGC: real environments (bedroom, closet, dressing room, coffee shop, kitchen,
  sidewalk) perform better than studio sets because they read as authentic
  ([Apiway](https://apiway.ai/blog/how-to-generate-ai-ugc-tiktok-ads), [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/ugc-for-fashion-brands/))

### 1.2 Camera Work: Full-Body Framing, Rotation, Detail Close-Ups

**Camera height and angle:**
- Camera lens positioned at **waist level** (belly button / navel height) — this
  is the golden rule. Only this point ensures zero perspective distortion of the
  figure ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2))
- Phone strictly perpendicular to the floor — no tilting toward or away from the
  subject ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2))
- Top-down shooting enlarges the head and shoulders by 15–20% and shortens legs
  ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2))
- Bottom-up shooting elongates legs but creates a massive chin and heavy hips
  ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2))
- Camera height around chest level with a short telephoto (85–135mm equivalent)
  reduces distortion of legs and torsos for on-model photography
  ([Photostudio](https://photostudio.io/blog/on-model-photography-guide))

**Distance:**
- Stand 2–2.5 meters away from the camera. Closer than 1.5m causes wide-angle
  distortion (fisheye effect on hips/shoulders) ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2))
- For full-body framing: frame from mid-thigh to just above the head, keeping
  enough space around the figure for motion adjustments ([TryFits AI](https://tryfitsai.com/blog/creator-tips-lights-camera-try-on-turning-ai-fashion-videos-into-your-most-engaging-content-yet/))

**Multi-angle coverage:**
- Front, back, 3/4 left, 3/4 right, side profile — each shows different fit cues
  ([Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f))
- Walk-around loop: model walks slowly left-to-right while camera circles
  180–360° ([Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f))
- Detail close-ups: neckline, cuff, hem, fabric texture (macro), zipper pull,
  stitching — for higher-price pieces this justifies cost and reduces anxiety
  ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce), [Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f))

**Movement passes:**
- **Turn**: full 360° rotation to show the garment from all angles
- **Walk**: shows drape, hem swing, how the fabric moves in motion
- **Sit/stand**: demonstrates rise and waist comfort — shows the piece does not
  pull or ride up ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce), [Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f))
- **Arm movement**: raise/lift arms to show sleeve fit and underarm clearance
  ([Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f))
- **Fabric stretch test**: gently pull to show elasticity and recovery (important
  for knits) ([Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f))
- **Backlit transparency pass**: silhouette shot against soft backlight to reveal
  sheerness ([Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f))

### 1.3 UGC Vibe vs Polished: What Differs

| Dimension | UGC Organic | Polished / Studio |
|---|---|---|
| **Setting** | Real environments — bedroom, closet, dressing room, car, sidewalk, coffee shop | Controlled studio, seamless backdrop, or curated location |
| **Lighting** | Natural window light, available light; no professional setup | 3-point lighting, softboxes, controlled color temperature |
| **Camera** | Handheld phone, vertical 9:16, slightly shaky is OK | Tripod or gimbal, stable framing, can be 4K |
| **Framing** | Mid-shot or selfie angle, imperfect composition | Full-body, centered, professionally composed |
| **Presenter** | Real person, casual, talking to camera like a friend | Model or trained presenter, polished delivery |
| **Editing** | Jump cuts, minimal color grading, burned-in captions | Clean transitions, color-graded, branded lower-thirds |
| **Polish level** | Deliberately unpolished — authenticity signal is what makes it perform | High production value for brand positioning |
| **Audio** | Conversational voiceover, trending sounds | Professional voiceover, licensed music |
| **Length** | 9–21s (TikTok), 15–30s (Reels) | 20–30s, longer for editorial |
| **ROAS** | 6.2x (documented) vs 0.8x for polished product-only | Lower direct-response ROAS, higher brand equity |
| **Cost** | $73–$130 per ad with AI; $300–$600 per human creator | $75–$530+ per ad with AI |

Sources: [Invideo](https://invideo.io/faq/ugc-try-on-ad-vs-cinematic-editorial-fashion-film-which/),
[Apiway](https://apiway.ai/blog/how-to-generate-ai-ugc-tiktok-ads),
[Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce)

**Key insight for AI generation**: TikTok's algorithm punishes content that looks
like an ad and rewards content that looks like a person. Polished studio creative
underperforms creator-style imagery by margins that often run 2–5x in CPM
([Apiway](https://apiway.ai/blog/how-to-generate-ai-ugc-tiktok-ads)). Resist the
urge to color-grade to a brand palette, overlay a logo, or add consistent
typography across shots — polish breaks the authenticity illusion
([Apiway](https://apiway.ai/blog/how-to-generate-ai-ugc-tiktok-ads)).

**When to use each:**
- **UGC organic**: Conversion/direct-response from paid social. The job is to stop
  the scroll and drive add-to-cart. This is the workhorse format.
- **Polished/studio**: Brand positioning, premium products, editorial campaigns.
  Build premium brand perception rather than drive immediate clicks.
- **Best practice**: Make both. At AI production costs, ship both in parallel and
  scale whichever wins ([Invideo](https://invideo.io/faq/ugc-try-on-ad-vs-cinematic-editorial-fashion-film-which/)).

### 1.4 Fit and Styling: How to Show Fit, Drape, Movement

**The three conversion questions** (from [Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce)):
1. *Will it fit me?* → Show the garment on a real body with stated height and size
2. *Will it look like the photo?* → Show the fabric in motion, in real light
3. *Will I actually wear it?* → Show styling — how it pairs with other items

**How to demonstrate fit:**
- Mention the model's **height and usual size** explicitly
- Note whether the piece **runs large, small, or true to size**
- Show **one realistic flaw** alongside the wins — honesty is the trust signal
  that beats the objection ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce))
- Show the garment **in motion**: a turn, a walk, a sit-down to show the piece
  does not pull or ride up ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce))
- Side-by-side of **two sizes** on the same body, or the same piece on **two
  different body types** — kills fit anxiety that drives returns ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce))

**How to show drape and movement:**
- Walk to show hem swing and fabric flow
- Stretch test for knits (gently pull to show elasticity and recovery)
- How light catches the fabric (especially for silk, satin, chiffon, knit textures)
- Quick cuts on stitching, fabric weave, zipper pull — makes quality tangible
  for higher-price pieces ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce))

**Styling for versatility:**
- One piece styled three ways: dressed up, dressed down, layered — sells
  versatility and raises AOV because the viewer mentally buys the whole look
  ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce))
- GRWM (Get Ready With Me) context places the product in a real-life scenario
  (date, work event, trip) — answers "when would I actually wear this?"
  ([Billo](https://billo.app/blog/ugc-for-fashion-brands/))

### 1.5 Before/After: How to Structure the Transformation

The **"Try-on Transformation"** is the single highest-performing format for fashion
on TikTok, consistently driving 18–32% higher add-to-cart rates
([Marketing News UK](https://www.marketing-news.uk/what-exact-tiktok-creative-format-increases-add-to-cart-rates-for-fashion-retailers)).

**Structure:**
1. **Hook (0–2s)**: Bold before/after clip or a clear visual promise
   ("Watch how this dress fits on different body types")
2. **Transformation**: Show the garment going on — from holding it up to wearing it
3. **Multiple angles**: Quick cuts of the same piece in motion — walking, sitting,
   close fabric shot
4. **Product tag**: On-screen product tag with size availability and price
5. **CTA**: "Tap to see sizes" or "Limited stock — add to cart"

**Key principles:**
- The transformation must be **visible and immediate** — the viewer sees the item
  on a real body, in motion, with a frictionless path to cart
- Include **at least two body types or sizes** in a single ad to increase
  perceived fit relevance ([Marketing News UK](https://www.marketing-news.uk/what-exact-tiktok-creative-format-increases-add-to-cart-rates-for-fashion-retailers))
- The **skeptic-to-convert** structure ("I was skeptical but...") reliably
  outperforms straight praise in apparel ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce))
- For AI generation: use **first-frame and last-frame conditioning** to lock the
  before/after states. Choose two similar images with the same theme — large
  differences trigger a shot switch ([WearView](https://www.wearview.co/blog/ai-video-of-person-wearing-outfit-from-photo))

### 1.6 Creator Reaction: How the Presenter Should React

**Authenticity rules** (from [Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/product-video-reviews-guide/),
[Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce)):
- Reactions should feel **spontaneous, not scripted**. Slight imperfections (ums,
  pauses, camera not perfectly centered) increase perceived authenticity
- **Genuine excitement** — the first try-on reaction is the most powerful moment
- **Honest commentary** on fit issues — mention if it runs large, small, or true to
  size; note the fabric feel, the stretch, whether it's see-through
- **One honest limitation** alongside the wins builds more trust than pure praise.
  Examples: "The material is thinner than expected but perfect for summer" or
  "Runs slightly large — size down if between sizes"
- Specificity beats generality: "The ruching hides everything" beats "flattering
  silhouette" ([VideoAI.me](https://videoai.me/blog/ugc-fashion-boutique-clothing-video))

**Reaction beats to include in a prompt:**
- Initial reaction to seeing/touching the fabric (unboxing moment)
- First reaction while putting it on (surprise, delight, or honest assessment)
- Movement reaction (how it feels to walk, sit, move in it)
- Final verdict (would they actually wear it, when, and why)

### 1.7 Lighting: What Flatters Both Product and Wearer

**Lighting setups:**

| Setup | Use Case | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Frontal natural daylight** | UGC, phone-shot, AI input photos | Face the window so light hits evenly from the front. Smooths skin tone, gives AI a clear image. ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2)) |
| **Window + reflector** | Phone-first favorite | Diffuse window light with a white reflector. Natural light looks authentic on mobile screens, reduces post-production color shifts. ([Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f)) |
| **Soft 3-point studio** | Polished/studio | Key: large softbox at 45° above camera height. Fill: reflector or soft light opposite key. Backlight: hair/rim light to separate model from background. ([Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f)) |
| **Cross-polarized** | Shiny embellishments, jewelry | Polarizing filters on lights and lens to remove specular highlights and reveal true texture. ([Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f)) |
| **Backlit transparency** | Sheer fabrics | Soft LED panel behind diffusion screen, silhouette exposure to reveal sheerness. ([Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f)) |

**Lighting principles:**
- **Soft, diffused, even** — reduces harsh shadows that distort fit and fabric
  appearance ([Coohom](https://www.coohom.com/article/lighting-tips-for-fitting-room-cam),
  [Intimates.live](https://intimates.live/video-try-on-breaking-boundaries-with-diverse-body-represent))
- CRI (Color Rendering Index) **above 90** for accurate fabric and skin tones
  ([Coohom](https://www.coohom.com/article/lighting-tips-for-fitting-room-cam))
- Warm white **3000–4000K** range — balances flattering skin tones with true
  clothing colors ([Coohom](https://www.coohom.com/article/lighting-tips-for-fitting-room-cam))
- Light from a **single defined direction** creates depth and makes fabric look
  three-dimensional. Even ambient light everywhere flattens fabric
  ([Picasso IA](https://blog.picassoia.com/create-virtual-try-on-photos-for-clothes))
- Natural daylight gives fabrics like silk and denim more realistic reflections
  than artificial light ([TryFits AI](https://tryfitsai.com/blog/creator-tips-lights-camera-try-on-turning-ai-fashion-videos-into-your-most-engaging-content-yet/))
- **Avoid**: harsh overhead lighting (creates dark circles under eyes, obscures
  waistline), backlighting (silhouettes the subject), and uneven lighting
  (exaggerates seam lines and shadowed folds on phone screens)
  ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2), [Asianwears](https://asianwears.com/virtual-try-on-prep-photo-and-video-assets-your-team-needs-f))

### 1.8 Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Top-down camera angle** | Enlarges head/shoulders 15–20%, shortens legs — AI takes distorted data as accurate | Camera at waist level, phone perpendicular to floor ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2)) |
| **Slouching / half-turned poses** | Obscures anatomical markers; AI can't determine figure symmetry, creates creases | Stand straight, shoulders squared, look straight ahead ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2)) |
| **Crossed arms / hands on waist** | Blocks chest and stomach area; AI can't fit the central part of the garment | Arms slightly away from body, 10–15 cm gap at waist ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2)) |
| **All-black clothing** | Absorbs light, kills shadows; AI sees a dark silhouette with no definition at knees/calves | Use neutral, muted shades (gray, beige, dusty pink, muted khaki) ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2)) |
| **Pure white clothing** | Overexposes, blends with wall | Choose a contrasting neutral base ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2)) |
| **Harsh overhead lighting** | Creates unflattering shadows under eyes/nose, obscures waistline | Use frontal natural light from a window, or soft diffused light at eye level ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2), [Coohom](https://www.coohom.com/article/lighting-tips-for-fitting-room-cam)) |
| **Backlighting** | Silhouettes the subject, phone auto-lowers exposure | Window should be in front of, not behind, the subject ([MioLook](https://miolook.com/en/how-to-take-photo-virtual-try-on-2)) |
| **Over-polishing UGC content** | Breaks the authenticity illusion; reads as an ad, gets scrolled past | Don't color-grade to brand palette, don't overlay logos, don't add consistent typography ([Apiway](https://apiway.ai/blog/how-to-generate-ai-ugc-tiktok-ads)) |
| **Scripted creator reactions** | Reads like a brand produced it — kills trust | Brief context, not execution; let styling/reactions/commentary be the creator's ([Billo](https://billo.app/blog/ugc-for-fashion-brands/)) |
| **Only showing one body type** | Leaves most potential buyers unanswered — "will this look good on someone like me?" | Include at least two body types/sizes per ad ([Marketing News UK](https://www.marketing-news.uk/what-exact-tiktok-creative-format-increases-add-to-cart-rates-for-fashion-retailers)) |
| **No movement — static pose only** | Doesn't show drape, hem swing, how fabric behaves in motion | Always include a turn, walk, or sit-down ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce)) |
| **Cluttered / busy background** | Distracts from product; for AI input photos, confuses the model | Plain, neutral-colored backdrop for studio; real but uncluttered environment for UGC ([Alsett](https://alsett.com/creating-high-quality-try-on-videos-expert-guide/), [Vensa](https://vensa.app/best-practices)) |
| **Shooting too wide** | Face preservation fails hardest when face occupies a small fraction of the image | If you need both full look and face, consider two clips ([WearView](https://www.wearview.co/blog/ai-video-of-person-wearing-outfit-from-photo)) |
| **Asking AI to both preserve garment AND create editorial look** | Fidelity and style conflict in one step | Split into stages: standardize product first, then push style ([OpenCreator](https://opencreator.io/blog/ai-virtual-model-try-on-workflow-opencreator)) |

## 2. Product Review (Presenter Giving an Opinion)

### Core Format Definition

A product review video shows a person demonstrating a product in use and giving
their honest opinion on how it performs. In ad format (15–30s), it compresses the
review into a hook → demo → verdict structure. The format's primary job is to
provide **visual proof** that the product does what it claims, delivered by a
credible, relatable person.

### 2.1 Review Structure: First Impression → Testing → Verdict

**The universal spine** (from [Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/product-video-ad-formats-best-practices),
[AdvertStar](https://advertstar.net/2025/10/13/creating-video-product-reviews-a-complete-guide-to-plan-shoot-and-optimize/)):

Almost every winning product review follows a **three-part spine: hook, body, CTA**.
The proportions shift by format, but the skeleton is constant.

**For short-form ads (15–30s)** — the 5-beat structure
([UGC Vids AI](https://ugcvids.ai/blog/how-to-script-a-30-second-ugc-ad)):

| Beat | Timing | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | 0–2s | Pattern-interrupt + pre-qualify the viewer |
| 2. Problem | 2–7s | Name the pain in the viewer's language |
| 3. Demo | 7–22s | Show the product working — proof, not claim (longest beat) |
| 4. Payoff | 22–27s | State the specific, measurable outcome |
| 5. Soft CTA | 27–30s | Tell them what to do next without pressure |

**For 15s ads**: Compress the demo to 8s and combine payoff + CTA into 3–4s total.
Keep all five beats — viewers feel when one is missing.

**For 60s ads**: Stretch the demo from 15s to 35–40s. Do NOT add a second hook,
problem, or extra CTAs. The structure is fixed; only the demo scales.

**For full-length reviews** (YouTube, PDP) — the 7-beat arc
([AdvertStar](https://advertstar.net/2025/10/13/creating-video-product-reviews-a-complete-guide-to-plan-shoot-and-optimize/)):

1. **Hook (0–8s)**: State the audience and the core decision they need to make
2. **Quick verdict (8–15s)**: Share your headline take right away to reward viewers
3. **What's in the box (15–30s)**: Fast, clean overhead shots; highlight surprises
4. **Core features in context**: Demonstrate with scenarios, not just specs
5. **Pros and cons**: Be specific and quantify where possible
6. **Alternatives**: Who should buy X instead of Y and why
7. **CTA**: Next step — chapters, links, coupon, or related reviews

**The Problem-Solution-Outcome arc** (for customer-sourced reviews,
[Image Media Lab](https://www.imagemedialab.com/video-blog/how-to-optimize-customer-video-review-strategy)):
- Problem: What specific issue did the customer have?
- Solution: How did the product address it?
- Outcome: What measurable result did they get?

### 2.2 Presenter Stance: Authentic Opinion vs Scripted Endorsement

**The golden rule**: A review must feel like a real person sharing a genuine
experience, not a brand reading a script ([Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/product-video-reviews-guide/)).

**What makes it authentic:**
- **Real environments**: A customer in their actual kitchen, living room, or
  backyard is far more convincing than a studio set. A little background noise
  is fine ([Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/product-video-reviews-guide/))
- **Unscripted talk**: Genuine excitement, natural pauses, "ums" or "ahs" are
  part of the package — shows this is a real person, not an actor
  ([Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/product-video-reviews-guide/))
- **Balanced feedback**: A review that mentions a minor learning curve ("It took
  me 10 minutes to set up…") often **boosts credibility** by proving the review
  isn't fake ([Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/product-video-reviews-guide/),
  [WiserNotify](https://wisernotify.com/blog/product-review-videos/))
- **Slight imperfections**: Shaky camera, imperfect framing, natural lighting —
  these signal "real person, not paid actor"
  ([AdCreate](https://adcreate.com/blog/social-proof-in-video-ads),
  [MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/customer-reviews-paid-ads/))

**The "unpolished proof" phenomenon**: MHI Media tested two versions —
a professional creator saying "This product is amazing!" vs a real customer
(shaky camera, natural light) saying "Okay, I was super skeptical, but my skin
actually looks better." The unpolished version achieved **2.1x higher CTR and
1.7x higher conversion** because imperfection signals authenticity
([MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/customer-reviews-paid-ads/)).

**When scripting is appropriate**: "Never script" is not an absolute rule.
Financial, health, environmental, and other regulated claims may need controlled
wording. Even then, the creator must not present an approved line as their personal
result unless it is true ([Space Ads](https://www.spaceads.agency/blog/ugc-ads-how-to-brief-source-and-run-creator-content)).

**Who should be on camera** (data from Whatmore's study of 595 ecommerce videos,
[Whatmore](https://www.whatmore.ai/blog/ecommerce-video-playbook-every-product-category/)):
- **Founder** on camera: 1.23x engagement (highest, but small sample n=29)
- **Influencer**: 1.04x (slightly above median)
- **Hands-only** (no face): 0.93x (weakest — faceless product-in-hands is
  comfortable to make and weak to watch)
- The founder brings genuine conviction, product knowledge, and a face the audience
  associates with the brand itself

### 2.3 Product Interaction: Hands-On Demonstration During Review

**Show, don't tell** — this is the single most repeated principle across all
sources. A hand actually using the product beats a voiceover claiming it works
([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/product-video-ad-formats-best-practices)).

**Demonstration types** (from [Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/product-video-reviews-guide/)):
- **Unboxing**: Shows packaging, what's included, the "first impression" moment
- **Product in use**: A customer using the blender, wearing the boots, assembling
  the shelf — the product solving a real problem in real time
- **Before-and-after**: Incredibly powerful for skincare, cleaning products, or
  home organization — seeing the result is the ultimate convincer
- **Feature walk-through**: Each video demonstrates one clearly identifiable
  product feature ([Amazon SP Creative Toolkit](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/01/AdProductsWebsite/images/guides/SPV_Creative_Toolkit_Downloadable_vF_A20M_version_2.pdf))

**The demonstration beat**: Name the specific on-screen action that proves the
claim — pour, apply, fold, click, before/after toggle. UGC without a demo is just
a talking head ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-video-ads-for-ecommerce)).

**Product visibility**: Product fills 50%+ of the frame, product shown in real use,
a person interacts with the product (strongly recommended for CTR)
([Amazon SP Creative Toolkit](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/01/AdProductsWebsite/images/guides/SPV_Creative_Toolkit_Downloadable_vF_A20M_version_2.pdf)).

**B-roll**: Capture more than you think you need. Shoot a variety of angles —
wide establishing, medium hand interactions, and macro details. Use consistent
white balance so shots match in post ([AdvertStar](https://advertstar.net/2025/10/13/creating-video-product-reviews-a-complete-guide-to-plan-shoot-and-optimize/)).

### 2.4 Visual Evidence: How to Show Proof of Claims Visually

**Proof, not claim** — the demo beat is the longest because it is the most
important. Show the product doing the thing, not telling
([UGC Vids AI](https://ugcvids.ai/blog/how-to-script-a-30-second-ugc-ad)).

**Visual proof methods:**
- **Before/after**: Two states side by side, no narration needed — the proof
  carries the ad, works with sound off ([UGCGen](https://ugcgen.ai/ugc-ads))
- **In-action demonstration**: The product solving a problem in real time —
  "dirty → product applied → result in one continuous clip"
  ([Amazon SP Creative Toolkit](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/01/AdProductsWebsite/images/guides/SPV_Creative_Toolkit_Downloadable_vF_A20M_version_2.pdf))
- **Specific results with numbers**: "I lost 14 pounds in 6 weeks" is more
  believable than "It really works" ([AdCreate](https://adcreate.com/blog/social-proof-in-video-ads))
- **Comparison**: Show the product next to the alternative it replaces
  ([AdvertStar](https://advertstar.net/2025/10/13/creating-video-product-reviews-a-complete-guide-to-plan-shoot-and-optimize/))
- **Wear/durability test**: "This bottle survived 12 throws onto tile"
  ([Superdeal](https://superdeal.io/blog/ugc-videos))

**The key insight**: The best reviews demonstrate the product in action, rather
than just featuring a customer talking to camera with the product sitting on a
shelf ([Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/product-video-reviews-guide/)).

### 2.5 Pros/Cons Framing: How to Mention Benefits Without a Hard Sell

**The one-con rule**: Including one honest con in a video makes every pro in it
more believable ([WiserNotify](https://wisernotify.com/blog/product-review-videos/),
[AdvertStar](https://advertstar.net/2025/10/13/creating-video-product-reviews-a-complete-guide-to-plan-shoot-and-optimize/)).

**How to frame pros:**
- **Specific, not generic**: "My hormonal acne cleared up in 3 weeks after 5 years
  of trying prescription creams" beats "Great product, love it!"
  ([MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/customer-reviews-paid-ads/))
- **Quantify where possible**: Specific numbers, timeframes, measurable outcomes
  ([AdvertStar](https://advertstar.net/2025/10/13/creating-video-product-reviews-a-complete-guide-to-plan-shoot-and-optimize/))
- **One benefit per clip**: Each clip should make a single argument (saves time,
  looks premium, solves this problem). Trying to say everything says nothing
  ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-video-ads-for-ecommerce))
- **Tie to the hook's promise**: Pick the one benefit that matches the hook and
  prove it. Resist the urge to list every feature
  ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/product-video-ad-formats-best-practices))

**How to frame cons:**
- State them **plainly next to pros** — the criticism is what makes the praise
  convert ([WiserNotify](https://wisernotify.com/blog/product-review-videos/))
- Frame as a **minor learning curve or honest limitation**: "It took me 10 minutes
  to set up…" or "The material is thinner than expected but perfect for summer"
  or "Runs slightly large — size down if between sizes"
  ([Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/product-video-reviews-guide/), [VideoAI.me](https://videoai.me/blog/ugc-fashion-apparel-ai-video-guide))
- **"I was skeptical, but…"** structure — pre-empts doubt before the viewer feels
  it, reliably outperforms straight praise
  ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce),
  [MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/customer-reviews-paid-ads/))

### 2.6 Trust Signals: What Makes a Review Feel Genuine vs Paid Promotion

**Hierarchy of social proof** (from MHI Media's analysis of 800+ DTC ad campaigns,
[MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/customer-reviews-paid-ads/)):

| Social Proof Type | Avg. CTR | Avg. CVR | Trust Score (1-10) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verified customer review (video) | 3.4% | 4.8% | 9.2 |
| Verified customer review (text + photo) | 2.9% | 4.1% | 8.7 |
| UGC testimonial | 2.8% | 3.9% | 8.3 |
| Aggregated ratings ("4.8/5 from 2,000 reviews") | 2.3% | 3.2% | 7.4 |
| Expert endorsement | 2.1% | 3.0% | 7.8 |
| Generic testimonial quote | 1.7% | 2.4% | 6.1 |
| Brand claims (no proof) | 1.4% | 2.0% | 4.9 |

**What creates trust:**

1. **Demographic matching**: The testimonial subject should look and sound like
   your target customer. A 22-year-old presenting a product for 50-year-olds
   creates cognitive dissonance ([AdCreate](https://adcreate.com/blog/social-proof-in-video-ads))

2. **Specificity beats generality**: "I lost 14 pounds in 6 weeks" is more
   believable than "It really works" ([AdCreate](https://adcreate.com/blog/social-proof-in-video-ads))

3. **Imperfection signals authenticity**: Slightly shaky camera, natural lighting,
   imperfect framing — these subconsciously communicate "real person, not paid
   actor" ([MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/customer-reviews-paid-ads/))

4. **"I was skeptical too"**: Mirrors the viewer's internal dialogue, creates
   instant connection and relatability ([MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/customer-reviews-paid-ads/))

5. **Timeframe-anchored hooks**: "Day 14 of this pre-workout, real review" or
   "I've been gatekeeping this for three months" — timeframe + result tied to a
   fixed window creates credibility ([Superdeal](https://superdeal.io/blog/ugc-videos))

6. **"Verified Purchase" badge**: Including reviewer first name + last initial
   and a verified badge reinforces legitimacy ([MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/customer-reviews-paid-ads/))

7. **One honest con**: A flaw stated plainly is what makes the rest believable.
   A review with zero downsides persuades no one
   ([WiserNotify](https://wisernotify.com/blog/product-review-videos/))

8. **Skeptic-to-convert structure**: "I didn't think this would actually work,
   but..." outperforms straight praise because it pre-empts the doubt the viewer
   already feels ([MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/customer-reviews-paid-ads/),
   [Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-video-ads-for-ecommerce))

9. **Disclosure and transparency**: FTC disclosure (#ad, "Paid partnership" overlay)
   paradoxically increases trust — viewers know the relationship is disclosed, so
   the content feels more honest ([Superdeal](https://superdeal.io/blog/ugc-videos),
   [Space Ads](https://www.spaceads.agency/blog/ugc-ads-how-to-brief-source-and-run-creator-content))

**What kills trust:**
- Overly polished production that reads as a commercial ([Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/product-video-reviews-guide/))
- No real testing — just talking head with product on shelf ([Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/product-video-reviews-guide/))
- Generic claims without specifics ([MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/customer-reviews-paid-ads/))
- No cons or limitations mentioned ([WiserNotify](https://wisernotify.com/blog/product-review-videos/))
- Fake reactions or results the creator did not achieve ([Space Ads](https://www.spaceads.agency/blog/ugc-ads-how-to-brief-source-and-run-creator-content))

### 2.7 Timing: How to Fit a Review in 10–15 Seconds

**Platform-specific length guidance:**

| Platform | Optimal Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 9–21s (best: 15–25s) | Hook by 1.5s; strongest performance in 15–25s band |
| Reels | 15–30s (sweet spot: 20s) | Hook by 1.5s; aesthetic matters more |
| Meta Feed | 15–30s | Front-load first 3–5s; most viewers never reach the end |
| YouTube Shorts | 15–35s | Hook by 2.0s; slightly slower pacing tolerated |

Sources: [Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/product-video-ad-formats-best-practices),
[UGC Vids AI](https://ugcvids.ai/blog/how-to-script-a-30-second-ugc-ad),
[Invideo](https://invideo.io/faq/what-is-the-best-length-for-a-ugc-ad-on-instagram-reels/),
[Spocket](https://www.spocket.co/blogs/youtube-shorts-for-product-reviews)

**For a 10–15s review ad**, the compressed structure is:

```
Hook (0-2s):     Pattern-interrupt + pre-qualify
Problem (2-5s):  Name the pain in the viewer's words
Demo (5-10s):    Show the product working — the proof
Payoff+CTA (10-15s): Specific outcome + one action
```

**Key timing principles:**
- **The hook decides everything**: The first 1–3 seconds carry most of the
  performance variance. Over-invest here
  ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce))
- **Lead with the verdict, not the introduction**: Share your headline take right
  away to reward viewers ([AdvertStar](https://advertstar.net/2025/10/13/creating-video-product-reviews-a-complete-guide-to-plan-shoot-and-optimize/))
- **Show product in first 2 seconds**: Don't save the reveal for the end
  ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/product-video-ad-formats-best-practices))
- **Change something every 3–5 seconds**: New angle, graphic, or demo to maintain
  attention ([AdvertStar](https://advertstar.net/2025/10/13/creating-video-product-reviews-a-complete-guide-to-plan-shoot-and-optimize/))
- **Quick-clips are the worst duration**: Quick-clip (under ~7s) performed at
  0.76x vs short video at 1.00x. Give the video enough room to actually say
  something ([Whatmore](https://www.whatmore.ai/blog/ecommerce-video-playbook-every-product-category/))
- **Medium videos outperform**: 1.17x vs 1.00x for short. The lesson is not "make
  long videos" — it's "give the video enough room to say something"
  ([Whatmore](https://www.whatmore.ai/blog/ecommerce-video-playbook-every-product-category/))

### 2.8 CTA Approach: How Review Ads Convert

**Single action, single destination**: One ask. "Tap to shop," "Link in bio,"
"Get yours before it sells out." Spoken and on-screen. Never end on a fade-to-logo
with no instruction — that's where conversions leak out
([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/product-video-ad-formats-best-practices)).

**Soft CTA for review ads**: Review ads work best with **permission, not pressure**.
The CTA should feel like a recommendation from a friend, not a sales push
([UGC Vids AI](https://ugcvids.ai/blog/how-to-script-a-30-second-ugc-ad)).

**CTA patterns by review type:**

| Review Type | CTA Style | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Testimonial | Soft, personal recommendation | "If you've tried everything and nothing's worked, just try this one." |
| Problem-solution | Direct but empathetic | "Tap to shop the bundle." |
| Before/after | Visual proof + curiosity | "See the full results at the link." |
| Comparison | Informative | "Check the comparison at the link." |
| Skeptic-to-convert | Low pressure | "I got mine from [website], there's a discount code: [CODE]." |

Sources: [UGC Vids AI](https://ugcvids.ai/blog/how-to-script-a-30-second-ugc-ad),
[MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/customer-reviews-paid-ads/)

**CTA timing**: The CTA goes in the **last 2–3 seconds**. Adding mild urgency or a
reason-to-act-now lifts click-through, but a clear single CTA beats a clever vague
one every time ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/product-video-ad-formats-best-practices)).

**Common CTA mistake**: Bolting on a soft second CTA in the last 2 seconds —
usually "follow for more" or "comment your favourite." It dilutes the click and
tells the algorithm to optimize for the wrong thing
([Spark UGC](https://www.sparkugc.com/resources/ugc-ad-script-structure-template)).

**Platform-specific CTA:**
- TikTok: Softer CTA like "Learn more at the link in bio" feels more native
- Meta: "Shop Now" button is the workhorse for e-commerce
- The CTA should map to the metric you're billed against — if paying for
  purchases, the CTA is purchase
  ([Spark UGC](https://www.sparkugc.com/resources/ugc-ad-script-structure-template))

### 2.9 Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Overly positive / no cons** | A review with zero downsides persuades no one; reads as fake | Include one honest limitation alongside the wins ([WiserNotify](https://wisernotify.com/blog/product-review-videos/)) |
| **No real testing / product on shelf** | Talking head with product sitting on a shelf provides no visual proof | Show the product in use — unboxing, in-action, before/after ([Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/product-video-reviews-guide/)) |
| **Too polished / scripted** | Reads as a paid advertisement, breaks trust | Real environments, unscripted talk, natural pauses, "ums" are part of the package ([Yotpo](https://www.yotpo.com/blog/product-video-reviews-guide/)) |
| **Generic claims** | "It works great!" is meaningless | Specific details: "My hormonal acne cleared up in 3 weeks" ([MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/customer-reviews-paid-ads/)) |
| **No hook in first 2s** | Viewers decide in under a second whether to keep watching | Pattern-interrupt, curiosity gap, or specific pain call-out ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/product-video-ad-formats-best-practices)) |
| **Too short (< 7s)** | Quick-clips are the worst duration — not enough room to say anything | Give the video enough room to demonstrate and deliver a verdict ([Whatmore](https://www.whatmore.ai/blog/ecommerce-video-playbook-every-product-category/)) |
| **Spec dumps without context** | Lists features without showing what numbers mean in real life | Demonstrate with scenarios, not just specs ([AdvertStar](https://advertstar.net/2025/10/13/creating-video-product-reviews-a-complete-guide-to-plan-shoot-and-optimize/)) |
| **Multiple CTAs** | Dilutes the click, tells algorithm to optimize wrong thing | One action, one destination, spoken once and captioned once ([Spark UGC](https://www.sparkugc.com/resources/ugc-ad-script-structure-template)) |
| **No demographic matching** | 22-year-old presenting a product for 50-year-olds creates cognitive dissonance | Match presenter to target customer ([AdCreate](https://adcreate.com/blog/social-proof-in-video-ads)) |
| **Fake controversy / fabricated reactions** | Attracts wrong audience, damages trust | Avoid fake controversy; test real proof, demonstration, objection, offer ([Space Ads](https://www.spaceads.agency/blog/ugc-ads-how-to-brief-source-and-run-creator-content)) |
| **Hidden disclosure** | FTC treats hidden material connections as deceptive advertising | Clear, prominent disclosure on screen and early — #ad, "Paid partnership" overlay ([Space Ads](https://www.spaceads.agency/blog/ugc-ads-how-to-brief-source-and-run-creator-content), [Superdeal](https://superdeal.io/blog/ugc-videos)) |
| **Cinematic production for review ads** | Cinematic finished last at 0.64x engagement — worst production style | Use casual-UGC or aesthetic-edited style instead ([Whatmore](https://www.whatmore.ai/blog/ecommerce-video-playbook-every-product-category/)) |
| **Silent video (no voiceover)** | Silent videos engaged at 0.90x vs 1.09x with voice — voice wins in every category | Add a voice — founder talking to camera, creator narrating, or quick spoken line over B-roll ([Whatmore](https://www.whatmore.ai/blog/ecommerce-video-playbook-every-product-category/)) |
| **Ending on logo fade with no CTA** | That's where conversions leak out | End with one clear action, spoken and on-screen ([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/product-video-ad-formats-best-practices)) |

## Cross-Cutting Findings (Both Formats)

### The One Rule That Never Breaks: Add a Voice

Across 595 ecommerce videos tested by Whatmore, videos with a voiceover or
on-camera speech engaged at **1.09x vs 0.90x** for silent videos — and voice won
in **every single category** tested. Nothing else in the study was this consistent.
"Voice" does not have to be a scripted studio voiceover — a founder talking to
camera, a creator narrating as they unbox, or a quick spoken "okay, here's why I
actually like this" over B-roll all count. The mechanism is not production value
— it is that a human voice gives the viewer a reason to stay, a point being made,
a person to react to. ([Whatmore](https://www.whatmore.ai/blog/ecommerce-video-playbook-every-product-category/))

### Academic Evidence on Review Video Characteristics

A peer-reviewed study analyzing 13,840 electronic product review videos on YouTube
(found: brighter videos, higher visual dynamics, reviewer faces visible, more
positive facial emotions, lower voice pitch, and faster speech rates are all
associated with higher perceived helpfulness of review videos)
([SAGE/POMS](https://sage.cnpereading.com/doi/10.1111/poms.13969)):

- **Brightness and visual dynamics** increase perceived helpfulness
- **Featuring reviewer's face** in review videos increases perceived helpfulness
- **More positive facial emotions** are associated with higher helpfulness
- **Lower voice pitch** is associated with higher perceived helpfulness
- **Faster speech rates** are associated with higher perceived helpfulness

### Hook Frameworks (10 Patterns)

From Superdeal's analysis of 25 UGC video patterns
([Superdeal](https://superdeal.io/blog/ugc-videos)):

| Pattern | Example | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern interrupt | "Day 1 vs. Day 30, no filter." | Transformation, before/after |
| Problem-solution | "If you wake up at 3am, this fixed it for me." | Supplements, wellness |
| Direct address | "If you have a kid under three, watch this." | Parenting, baby gear |
| Negative claim | "Most people use these wrong, here's the right way." | Demo, how-to |
| Curiosity gap | "8 weeks of this serum, receipts." | Beauty, fitness |
| Social proof open | "I've been gatekeeping this for three months." | Beauty, lifestyle |
| Number-led | "5 moves with one band, beginner-friendly." | Fitness, instructional |
| Question-led | "What did they send this month?" | Unboxing, subscription |
| Confession | "I've wanted this espresso machine for two years." | Lifestyle, premium goods |
| Demonstration-first | "Three meals in 12 minutes, let me show you." | Demo, food, productivity |

Pattern-interrupt and curiosity-gap hooks have the highest 3-second hold rate;
social-proof opens have the highest CTR-to-click conversion among viewers who hold.

### Creative Volume Is the Real Bottleneck

Both formats share the same production challenge: **creative fatigue is fast**
(5–14 days on TikTok), and a single hero video won't carry a season. To keep a
healthy ad account, plan for **15–30 distinct variations per month**, each with a
different person, hook, setting, or angle. The hook is the main experiment —
write five hooks per angle and treat them as separate ads
([Sepia](https://sepia-lab.com/en/blog/ugc-for-fashion-ecommerce)).
